The problem

Almost every enterprise AI use case turns out to be a document use case.

Strip the ambition out of any enterprise AI roadmap and you find the same thing underneath: a contract, a claim, a certificate, a statement, a submission. The decision is downstream. The document is the bottleneck. And these documents don't behave — they arrive as scans, as email attachments, as forty-page PDFs where the number you need lives in a footnote of appendix C.

Low volume, high sensitivity, zero tolerance for a wrong field.

Pilots rarely fail because a model can't read a page. They fail in the six feet between a correct extraction and someone actually acting on it.

That gap is not a modelling problem. It's exception handling, confidence thresholds, reviewer workload, retries, system-of-record writeback, audit evidence, and the twelve document variants nobody mentioned in the scoping call. It is engineering work, done next to the people who own the process.

Why embedding beats buying

A product has to be right for everyone. We only have to be right for you.

Extraction platforms are genuinely good now, and we build on that. But a platform is designed for the average document and the average workflow, and no enterprise operates an average workflow. What's left over — the variants, the exceptions, the handoffs, the evidence trail — is exactly where the return lives, and it's the part no vendor can ship you in a box.

Scaling across the enterprise

The second workflow should cost a fraction of the first. Then the tenth.

Most enterprises don't have an AI problem, they have a hundred document problems in twelve departments, each too small to justify its own vendor. The reason to build a shared extraction capability once is that everything after it gets cheap. Our job is to make the first one work, and then make ourselves progressively less necessary.

01

Land

Weeks 1–6

One workflow, one department, one measurable number — cycle time, touch rate, cost per document. Our engineers work from your side of the firewall against your real backlog, not a sample set.

02

Harden

Weeks 6–14

Evaluation harness, confidence thresholds, exception queues, monitoring, runbooks. This is the step that separates a pilot that survives contact with volume from one that quietly gets switched off.

03

Extend

Quarter 2 onward

Adjacent workflows reuse the same ingestion, schema library, review console and audit trail. New document types are a configuration and an eval set, not a new project with a new vendor.

04

Hand over

Continuous

Your engineers pair with ours from week one and take ownership deliberately. We shift to advising the next department, or we step back entirely. Both are a success.
